# Open-Source Landscape Research — Rules-as-Code, RegTech, Legal Knowledge Graphs

Methodology: targeted review of each project's official site/repository (name, license,
architecture) as of this research pass. Star counts and activity levels are approximate
and drift quickly — treat as directional, re-check before citing externally.

| Project | URL | License | Activity (approx.) | Architecture | Strengths | Weaknesses / fit for PermitGraph |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Observations specific to PermitGraph's USP (change detection → impact analysis)

No actively maintained, widely adopted open-source project was found that combines **all** of:
1. Ingesting primary regulatory source documents with OCR + provenance,
2. Producing versioned, declarative, testable rules,
3. Maintaining an **approval dependency graph**, and
4. Performing **semantic diff between regulation versions** connected to **impact analysis** against a business/application graph.

Individual pieces exist in isolation (OpenFisca for parametric rule versioning, Akoma Ntoso for document structure, Drools/Camunda for execution/orchestration, assorted "legal NLP" research repos for diffing). **PermitGraph's contribution is the connective tissue**: a provenance-first regulatory data model, a small rules-as-code DSL, a dependency graph, and a change→impact pipeline wired together end-to-end — not a novel rule-execution algorithm.

### What we explicitly do NOT copy
- We do not vendor Drools/OpenL/Camunda/Temporal into the MVP (infrastructure cost not justified yet; documented as roadmap options).
- We do not claim Akoma Ntoso or LegalRuleML conformance; we take inspiration from their structuring ideas only.
- We do not reuse any scraped third-party "Indian compliance dataset" verbatim (see `data-licensing.md`) — our real corpus is extracted directly from the three primary Acts supplied for this project, with our own provenance schema.
